Shortlist

AI candidate screening that reads a full hiring brief — not just a job description — and posts evidence-based scores directly into your ATS. Built for lean TA teams. No new tool for recruiters to learn.

Google Apps Script Gemini via LiteLLM ATS API (SmartRecruiters) Slack Google Sheets
The problem

Most AI matching tools compare resume text to job description text. They answer: does this CV look like this JD? Recruiters actually care about: would we realistically hire this person for this role?

The gap is widening. Candidates use AI to mirror JD keywords — ATS match scores look great, but recruiters still reject these candidates later. The score predicted keyword overlap, not hiring likelihood. And it can't tell the difference between a genuine five years of luxury retail experience and a resume that just says "luxury retail experience" with nothing to back it up.

Shortlist closes this gap by reading the full hiring context — the brief a recruiter has in their head, not just what's posted publicly.

How it works
1
Hiring brief generated from the JD
Shortlist pulls the job description from your ATS and generates a structured hiring brief — ideal candidate profile, must-haves, red flags, salary benchmarks, and first interview focus. This gives the model the same context a recruiter has in their head, not just what's posted publicly.
trigger: new job detected via ATS API
2
Recruiter reviews and approves the brief via Slack
The brief is posted to the recruiter as a Slack DM before scoring begins. They can approve it as-is, send corrections to get a revised version, or skip the role entirely. Screening only starts after explicit approval — no auto-scoring without human sign-off.
recruiter replies: "yes" to approve, or sends corrections → Gemini revises → V2 sent back
3
Candidates scored in batches
Shortlist fetches candidates in New status from SR, reads each resume, pulls screening answers (including salary expectation), and sends everything to Gemini for scoring against the approved brief. Runs in batches — 20 during office hours, 25 overnight — on a time-based trigger. State tracking ensures no candidate is scored twice.
day: every 30 min | night: every 1 hour | timezone-aware: SGT and IST
4
Results posted back to your ATS
Per candidate: star rating (1–5) and a full AI review comment are posted directly to the ATS via API. A tag is written to the candidate profile. The recruiter opens the ATS and sees everything already there — no new tool, no new login, no extra step. Built and tested on SmartRecruiters — adaptable to other ATS platforms with a reviews API.
POST /reviews-api · POST /candidates/{id}/tags · POST /candidates/{id}/fields
5
One consolidated Slack DM per batch
After each batch, the recruiter gets a single summary DM — not one ping per candidate. It lists shortlisted candidates with scores and direct SR profile links. If nothing was shortlisted, it says so clearly.
strong and review candidates only · direct link to SR profile
6
Audit trail written to Google Sheets
Every screening run is logged automatically — one row per candidate, one row per batch, one row per brief event. Three tabs: Candidate Log, Batch Summary, Brief Events. Designed so you can look back and ask whether the AI is actually producing good scores over time.
Candidate Log · Batch Summary · Brief Events

Legal and IT clearance

Shortlist sends candidate resumes to an external AI model via API. Resumes are PII — before running on real candidate data, the following need to be confirmed with your legal and IT teams.

Data privacy
Resumes contain personal data. Depending on your markets this falls under PDPA (Malaysia), PDPC (Singapore), GDPR (EU), or equivalent. Confirm whether your LiteLLM proxy keeps data within your organisation's infrastructure or routes to external servers. Confirm whether candidates need to be notified that AI screening is in use.
AI screening disclosure
Several jurisdictions are moving toward requiring disclosure when AI is used in hiring decisions. Even where not yet mandatory, a brief notice in your application flow is good practice and reduces risk. Shortlist is an assistant — it surfaces scores and reasoning, it does not make hiring decisions. That framing matters legally.
Bias and fairness
The scoring model may favour candidates from well-known companies, certain educational backgrounds, or standardised resume formats — not by intent, but by pattern. Before any pilot, agree with your team on what score distributions to track and what threshold would trigger a prompt revision. Document this before you start, not after.
IT and vendor approval
If your organisation has a vendor approval process, the LiteLLM endpoint and the underlying model provider both need clearance before real candidate data is sent through. The prototype is safe for testing with dummy data — real resumes require sign-off first.
The current build uses test data only. Legal and IT clearance documentation was prepared as part of the project scoping — covering data flow, retention, disclosure approach, and bias tracking plan — before any live pilot was proposed.
Honest limitations

Not tested at scale — built for lean teams, not enterprise volume. Scoring quality depends on brief quality — a vague JD produces a vague brief. Non-English resumes haven't been fully validated. There's a real bias risk: the model may score candidates from well-known companies higher simply due to name recognition.

The hardest part of building Shortlist isn't the code — it's tuning the scoring prompt to match what recruiters actually value. That's not engineering work. It's running it on real candidates, comparing to real decisions, and adjusting.
